# Ruler portrait prompts

Reusable prompts for making Realm Forge ruler portraits in any image generator. Drafts are in `assets/portrait-drafts/`.

**How to use:** paste **Frame + one Subject + one Style** together as a single prompt. Keep Frame and Style exactly the same every time, and change only the Subject, so the portraits look like one collection.

**Size:** tall 2:3, such as 1024×1536.

## Frame

```text
Vertical half-body character portrait for a fantasy grand-strategy game. Crop from above the head to the waist. Leave generous empty space above the head so that any crown or headdress sits fully inside the image and well clear of the top edge. Figure centered, body turned slightly three-quarters, looking at the viewer. Simple soft dark background with no scenery. No text, no logo, no border, no watermark. High collar, covered chest, no neckline, no cleavage, fully and modestly dressed.
```

## Card frames

The game's frames are aged parchment, to match the map. They aren't tinted: only the red wax seal at the top center is recolored per nation, so keep it red, round and plain. Paste the base prompt plus one design. (The first frames were grey metal, tinted whole; they're retired.)

```text
A single portrait card frame for a fantasy strategy game drawn in the style of an old printed map, seen perfectly flat and front-on with no perspective, perfectly symmetrical left to right. Tall rectangular shape in a 2:3 ratio, filling the image with a small even margin. The large central opening, about 85 percent of the frame width, is completely empty and pure white, with a clean straight inner edge on all four sides; nothing pokes inward into the opening. Everything outside the frame is pure white too. No person, no text, no letters, no numbers, no logo, no watermark, no shadow on the white areas. Crisp clean edges.
```

## Tips

- **Use a reference image.** If the service accepts one, upload a portrait you like and ask it to match the style.
- **Keep the crop the same.** Portraits are easier to fit in the game if the face sits in the same spot each time.
- **Check small.** Look at each portrait at about 130 px wide, because that's roughly how big it may appear on a game card.
